Financial secrecy is a structural driver of inequality, tax abuse, environmental destruction, corruption and democratic erosion. It allows wealth, assets and corporate activities to remain hidden from public scrutiny, reducing the capacity of states to mobilise resources for public services, social protection, care systems and climate action. The impacts are particularly severe in Global South countries.

## A · Automatic exchange of information

Automatic exchange of information for tax purposes is the key to ending offshore secrecy of wealth held in other countries and jurisdictions, including secrecy jurisdictions.

## B · Beneficial owners

Public registries of beneficial owners, to allow the taxation of wealth in all its forms and to end the impunity of the people behind human rights violations, environmental crimes, illicit financial flows and tax abuses.

## C · Country-by-country reporting

Public country-by-country reporting, to enable the collection of corporate taxes and to move towards unitary taxation, where each company is taxed as a single entity and taxing rights are distributed equitably, including through the United Nations Framework Convention on International Tax Cooperation.

## D · Data access and protection of whistleblowers

Public access to corporate and asset data: annual accounts, company filings, subsidiary accounts, tax incentives and subsidies, court records, fines and sanctions. And strong protection for whistleblowers who expose illicit, unethical or unsafe practices, as in the Panama Papers, LuxLeaks and SwissLeaks.

## E · Enabler professionals

Public disclosure of tax advice schemes and their automatic exchange across borders; money laundering laws that cover a wide set of assets and professionals; public registries of enablers; and public access to sanctions and fines.

## F · Finance industry transparency

Knowing the legal and beneficial owners of companies and entities held through private equity, private credit, hedge funds, non-bank lenders, insurance wrappers, real-estate investment trusts and public-private partnership contracts, in sectors from housing and care homes to public transport and infrastructure.

## G · Global Asset Registry

Global rules to declare the legal and beneficial owners of high-value and high-risk assets, including vessels, vehicles, aircraft, real estate, land, precious metals, art, digital assets, and forestry, fishing and mining rights, in interconnected registries with public access above a threshold of size and risk.
