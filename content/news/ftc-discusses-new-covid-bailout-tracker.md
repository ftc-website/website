On April 27, the FTC hosted a high-level panel to discuss our new [Covid Bailout Tracker](/ftc-reveals-new-covid-bailout-tracker/), which revealed a set of staggering findings regarding governmental funding responses to the pandemic. The panel discussion, which can be viewed in its entirety [here](https://youtu.be/b1PRklpq7wQ?t=4) (and below), touched on a range of topics, from global minimum corporate taxes to those left behind by pandemic spending, and how the prioritization of the corporate sector has exacerbated existing gender, economic, and social divisions around the world.

<div class="video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/b1PRklpq7wQ" title="Video" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>

Here are some of the highlights from the panelists, including key quotes and discussions during their presentations:  

## Matti Kohonen

  

#### FTC Director

  
*"What needs to happen is a global minimum corporate tax—and that needs to be negotiated at the United Nations, so that the process can be discussed equitably. And this has to include things like offshore income."*

<div class="video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/b1PRklpq7wQ?start=307&amp;end=501" title="Video" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>

## Hon. Irene Ovonji-Odida

#### Independent Commission for the Reform of International Corporate Taxation (ICRICT)

*"The important thing is coordination in developing countries, in alliance with civil society in the Global North, because history shows that unless you have direct engagement of developing countries, change will be hard to come by."*
<div class="video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/b1PRklpq7wQ?start=552&amp;end=1167" title="Video" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>

## Madhav Tipu Ramachandran

#### Centre for Budget and Governance Accountability (CBGA India)

*"Some accounting has been made for gender-specific responses , but in terms of many of the social differences that define India—caste and class and tribe—there has not been a wider accounting for these groups."*
<div class="video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/b1PRklpq7wQ?start=1168&amp;end=1584" title="Video" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>

## Chenai Mukumba

#### Policy Research and Advocacy Manager, Tax Justice Network Africa

*"Kenya and Sierra Leone performed much worse compared to the other countries in the Tracker... In Kenya, 95% of the resources the government allocated in response to the pandemic went to corporations—and almost 0% to the informal sector."*
<div class="video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/b1PRklpq7wQ?start=1585&amp;end=2158" title="Video" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>

## Ricardo Barrientos

#### Senior Economist, Central American Fiscal Studies Institute (ICEFI)

*"The pandemic was like a switch, and the switch went off on Dec. 31, 2020 in countries like El Salvador and Guatemala. The governments just shut off social protection programs, even while there's a third and fourth wave of the pandemic."*
<div class="video"><iframe src="https://www.youtube.com/embed/b1PRklpq7wQ?start=2185&amp;end=2750" title="Video" loading="lazy" allowfullscreen></iframe></div>
